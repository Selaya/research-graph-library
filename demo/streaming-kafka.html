Streaming pipeline (Kafka) — live mode: a real event feed, not a simulation

Two producers write to the orders topic; a three-partition consumer group polls it, enriches each batch, fans out to fraud check and inventory hold, AND-joins on a risk decision and notifies the customer. Nothing here is compiled from declared durations — a mock producer calls run.start/finish/spawn on a {mode:"live"} run every 330 ms, exactly as a WebSocket feed would. Partition 1's consumer stalls, its occupancy badge climbs while the others stay flat, and a group rebalance drains the backlog in one burst. The whole log is scrubbable: go back in time, then jump back to live.

  • Occupancy badges climb and drain. Each pulse is one poll batch. When partition 1 stops committing, tokens pile up on it — ×2, ×5, past ×10 — while partition 0 and partition 2 stay at zero or one. That badge is state().nodes[id].occupancy, and the feed itself drives every start/finish off the split live mode reports underneath it: waiting (arrived, nobody working on it) vs active (a consumer picked it up). The run is compiled with spawnOnStart: false, so a start() can never mint a batch out of nothing — only spawn() may.
  • Over budget. Each partition consumer declares a 600ms poll budget — two beats of headroom over the one beat a healthy commit takes. Live mode never schedules from a duration, but it does judge against it: once partition 1's held batch outruns its declared dwell, state().nodes[id].overBudget flips and the renderer dashes the box (data-over-budget) — while its ×N badge sits in its own gutter above the box, clear of the duration chip.
  • Runtime fan-out. run.spawn("topic", 2) drops a produce spike straight onto the topic, and run.spawn("cg.p1", 2) models a hot partition key — tokens minted by the feed, not by a compiled schedule.
  • The group opens up. The consumer group fulfil-svc starts collapsed (its in/out edges aggregated into meta-edges, ×3 on the box) and g.expand() reveals the three partition consumers just before the lag starts.
  • The rebalance beat. A camera move, a warn highlight with a pulse, a --smv-fill override via g.props() and a caption call it out; then one bulk run.finish("cg.p1") flushes the whole backlog downstream at once and you can watch the wave travel.
  • The AND-join is real, live. risk decision declares no join, so its two in-edges make it an implicit AND-join — and live mode honours it: a fraud verdict that lands before the inventory hold is held (it fills a pip and counts as waiting) until its partner arrives, then the pair merges into one releasable batch. Click any stage to caption its live split — that is g.on("nodeclick").
  • Time travel. ◀ 6s back is run.seek() — the tiles and the graph re-draw the past while the frontier keeps running underneath; Follow live is run.follow(). The transport bar under the graph scrubs the same log (it can never scrub past "now").

Legend — dashed pips under a node = live occupancy, ×N in the gutter above the box = several batches queued on one node, chip inside the top-right corner = the stage's declared data.duration (its budget at this feed's pace), a dashed box = a dwell that ran past that duration, two pips under risk decision = the implicit AND-join's two slots, slim bar under the graph = the pipeline preset's totals (declared sum, and the critical path through them).
