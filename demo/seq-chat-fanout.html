Group chat delivery — one send, three receivers

Client A sends one message into a room; the gateway checks who's actually online, persists the message once, then fans out on a single row — a live push to Client B and a queue entry for offline Client C, two balls in flight at once. A join waits for both receipts before A sees its double check. Later, when C reconnects, the queued message finally lands.

    What to look for

    • The gateway's ack fans out on one row: two balls leave together — a push to B, a queue entry in the store for C.
    • The store's queued ack usually arrives at the join first and waits; B's read receipt is what the join is really waiting on.
    • gwjoin is a declared join: "all" activation — it only fires once both branches have checked in.
    • After the gap, Client C's own reconnect kicks off a second, shorter delivery: drain the queue, render, receipt, ack.
    • Scrub the transport bar backwards — structural steps are snapshotted, so the diagram folds back up.
